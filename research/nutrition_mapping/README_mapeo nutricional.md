# Nutritional Mapping of the 323 Classes (Calorie Counter)

This mapping assigns each class in the dataset (`classes.json`) to a food item from the **USDA FoodData Central** database, together with all of its nutrients per 100 g, its **density** (g per cm³), and, when applicable, its **recipe** composition (composite dishes).

It enables converting what is observed in a photograph (volume in cm³ or portions) into **grams, calories, and macronutrients**.

Status: complete mapping (323/323 classes) verified on a 20-row sample batch (18/20 correct, 2 errors corrected). All 323 classes have a density value: 203 from USDA volume measures, 60 from published tables, 2 physical references, 43 geometrically calculated, and 15 recipe-calculated. **None remain pending weighing**; the 43 geometric values are flagged and carry an uncertainty of ±15–20% (see [Section 3](#3-density-g-per-cm³)). Pending: technical review of 48 nutrient rows.

## 2. How to Use the Mapping

### 2.1 Converting Volume to Grams

```
grams = volume (cm³) × density (g/cm³)
```

**Example (green bean / *judía verde*, `idx` 1):** density 0.528 g/cm³.

- 1 USDA cup = 236.6 cm³ → 236.6 × 0.528 = **125 g**. This matches the cup weight published by USDA (125 g), confirming consistency.

### 2.2 Converting Grams to Calories and Macronutrients

Using the class row in `nutrition_wide.csv`:

```
kcal       = grams / 100 × Energy (kcal)
protein    = grams / 100 × Protein (g)
carbs      = grams / 100 × Carbohydrate, by difference (g)
fat        = grams / 100 × Total lipid (fat) (g)
```

**Example (green bean, 125 g):**

| Nutrient | Per 100 g | In 125 g |
|---|---|---|
| Energy | 35 kcal | 43.8 kcal |
| Protein | 1.89 g | 2.4 g |
| Carbohydrates | 7.88 g | 9.9 g |
| Fat | 0.28 g | 0.35 g |

### 2.3 Full Pipeline (Photo → Result)

1. The vision model identifies the class and estimates the apparent volume (cm³) of each food item.
2. Look up the class in `nutrition_wide_densidad.csv`, multiply the volume by `densidad_g_cm3`, and obtain the weight in grams.
3. From the same row, multiply `grams / 100` by `kcal_100g` and by each nutrient.
4. If the volume corresponds to a portion in spoons or cups, use `nutrition_map.json` (`portions`) or USDA measures as an alternative.

If separate tables are preferred, `nutrition_wide.csv` and `densidad_alimentos.csv` can be joined on `idx`.

### 2.4 Composite Dishes

The 15 dishes with `fdc_data_type = receta` (rösti, coleslaw, salad, bolognese, risotto, tarts, etc.) have their nutrients calculated by summing their ingredients by weight. See [Section 4](#4-composite-dishes-recipes).

## 3. Density (g per cm³)

### What It Measures

The density in this mapping is the mass of the food per unit of **apparent volume**, i.e., including the air between pieces when the food is in a pile (cooked rice, salad, walnuts, flakes). For compact pieces (apple, meat, cheese), it is solid density.

This matters because a photograph measures apparent volume. Therefore, the density used corresponds to how the food appears on the plate.

### Where Each Value Comes From

Applied in the following priority order:

1. **USDA volume measure** (`fuente`: *USDA: medida de volumen de la entrada*), **203 classes**. USDA provides the weight of one cup or tablespoon of the food. We divide the weight by the volume (cup = 236.6 cm³; tablespoon = 14.8 cm³; teaspoon = 4.9 cm³, etc.). If multiple measures exist, we take the median, preferring unchopped/whole measures. Measures representing yields (`yield`), different physical forms (whole fruit vs. 1 cup sliced, block vs. grated), or out-of-range values (0.08–1.6 g/cm³) are discarded. Discarded example: hazelnut spread at 2.50 g/cm³, clearly a database error.
2. **Specific FAO/INFOODS entry** (`fuente`: *FAO/INFOODS Density DB v2*), **33 classes**: cooked pasta 0.59; cake 0.415; lasagna 1.04; soup 0.99 and thick soup 1.09; boiled potato (*patata cocida*) 0.59; almonds 0.46; pistachios 0.646; cooked shrimp 0.77; diced bell pepper 0.51; olives 0.65; bread 0.29 and white bread 0.42; among others.
3. **Table 4.2 of *Engineering Properties of Foods*** (`fuente`: *referencia tabulada: tabla 4.2*), **23 classes**: meats 1.055 (published range 1.05–1.07 for 74–84% water), lean fish 1.055, apple 0.85 (range 0.775–0.925), pear 1.00, and cheese 1.08–1.15 (range 1.08–1.23).
4. **Other published tables**, **4 classes**: whole potato (*patata entera*) 1.06 (USDA AMS specific gravity), beetroot 0.76 and cashews 0.56 (Engineering ToolBox), and nut spread 1.082 (USDA peanut butter measure, used as a proxy).
5. **Physical category reference** (`fuente`: *referencia* or *física*), **2 classes**: whole egg 1.03 and cream 0.99.
6. **Geometric calculation** (`fuente`: *cálculo geométrico*, `estado = calculada (geometría)`), **43 classes**: no published density exists in the form in which they are served. Computed as `ρ = mass / volume`, using the USDA portion mass (or a typical mass if USDA provides none) and the volume of a solid with typical commercial or portion dimensions: ellipsoid, sphere, cylinder, prism, pizza wedge, cone, or cup. **Dimensions were not measured from photographs**; they are typical sizes. Estimated uncertainty: ±15–20%. See the [full table](#geometrically-calculated-densities-43).
7. **Recipes** (`fuente`: *fórmula de mezcla sobre ingredientes*), **15 dishes**: mixture density via `ρ = 100 / Σ(gᵢ / ρᵢ)`, computed from each ingredient's density. Marked as *calculada (receta)*.

### Cross-Check Against FAO and Table 4.2

Recipe densities are computed from ingredient densities sourced from USDA, FAO, or Table 4.2. All 43 geometric values were checked against the expected physical range (0.25–1.20 g/cm³) and against FAO values for equivalent foods; all fell within range. The review also corrected keyword-matching misclassifications: `blueberries` had fallen under "blue cheese", `pineapple` under "nuts" (due to *pine*), `potato_salad` under "leafy greens", `pasta_in_butterfly_form_farfalle` under "butter" (due to *butterfly*), `quiche` under "cheese", and `salad_lambs_ear` under "lamb". All 168 changes are logged in `densidad_cambios.csv` with both previous and new values.

### Geometrically Calculated Densities (43)

For these 43 classes, no published density exists in the form in which they are served: whole fruit, vegetables, soft cheeses, and composite dishes. The USDA volume measure either does not exist or measures a different physical form (whole fruit vs. slices, block vs. grated, whole breast vs. diced). Therefore, density was calculated as:

```
ρ = portion mass / solid volume
```

- **Mass:** the USDA portion listed in `nutrition_map.json` ("Mass & Origin" column). If USDA does not provide a portion, a typical mass is used, as noted in the table.
- **Volume:** formula for a named solid (ellipsoid, sphere, cylinder, prism, pizza wedge, cone, or cup) using typical commercial or portion dimensions.
- **Dimensions were not measured from photographs:** they are typical sizes. The result is a **calculated** density, not a measurement. Estimated uncertainty: **±15–20%**.
- For a cup, 236.6 cm³ is used. For pizza, 30 cm (12") cut into 8 slices; thickness depends on type (thin 1.0 cm, with vegetables 1.4 cm, with ham 1.6 cm). For shredded meat, a conical pile of 10 cm diameter and 4 cm height. For persimmon (*caqui*) and kiwi, a typical size consistent with the mass (168 g and 75 g) was used: the USDA 2 1/2" measure for persimmon does not match 168 g, and the 140 g USDA portion for kiwi corresponds to a large fruit.
- Range across the 43 values: **0.259** (croissant) to **1.164** (praline).

### How to Improve Density Values

No densities remain pending weighing: all 323 classes have a value. The 43 geometric classes benefit most from **in-house measurement** (`medición propia`), because their dimensions are typical estimates rather than photograph measurements. Simply weigh a real portion (or measure volume by water displacement/graduated cylinder) and replace the value, recording `fuente` and `estado` as `medición propia`. The 265 classes with published or physical sources (203 USDA + 60 tables + 2 references) do not require weighing except for verification. The 15 recipes can be refined by weighing the finished dish.

## 4. Composite Dishes (Recipes)

Dishes without a faithful USDA entry (rösti, bolognese, risotto, coleslaw, salads, tarts, glucose drink…) are computed via `recetas.csv`:

- Each row is an ingredient with its USDA ID and its **grams per 100 g of prepared dish** (cooked, ready to eat).
- The grams in each recipe sum to 100 g.
- Nutrients: weighted sum of each nutrient by ingredient weight.
- A nutrient not reported by an ingredient counts as 0. This is accurate for sugars in an oil, but may **underestimate vitamins and minerals** when data are genuinely missing.
- Energy: if an ingredient only reports Atwater energy (957/958), it is used as standard energy (kcal).
- Typical portion (`porcion_g`): estimated, not measured.

To modify a recipe: edit `Utilidades/recetas.csv` (or the `RECETAS` list in `scripts/merge.py`) and regenerate (see [Section 6](#6-how-to-reproduce-or-modify)).

## 5. Mapping Criteria

Applied to all classes:

1. **Consumed state.** Vegetables, meats, fish, pasta, rice, and legumes are mapped cooked. Salads and fruit, raw. (Example: `chicken_breast` → roasted cooked chicken, not raw.)
2. **Class specification.** "Without salt" and "without dressing" are respected.
3. **Composite dishes.** Standard recipe per 100 g of prepared dish.
4. **Generic entries.** If the class does not specify preparation, the typical consumption preparation is used.
5. **Among two valid candidates, choose the most generic** (no brand, no rare variety).
6. **No brands.** Replaced by generic categories (e.g., DiGiorno → generic pizza).
7. **Complete entries.** USDA entries with incomplete macronutrients are discarded (e.g., Foundation grapefruit missing protein and fat).

These criteria are applied in `REVISION.md`.

## 7. Quality and Limitations

### What Is Verified

- **Energy coherence:** 316 of 323 classes satisfy kcal ≈ 4·P + 4·C + 9·F (±15%) with fiber correction. The remaining 7 are explained: wine, beer, and Aperol (alcohol is not included in the Atwater formula) and water (0 kcal).
- **Recipes:** all sum to 100 g.
- **Decisions:** all 323 classes have a final decision. None remain pending.
- **Measured error rate:** 2 errors in 20 verified rows (10%; 95% CI: 2.8%–30.1%). Both were corrected (`spring_roll_fried` and `bread_grain`).

### What Is Not Verified

- **Geometrically calculated densities:** 43 rows with `estado = calculada (geometría)`. **These are not measurements.** Mass comes from a USDA portion or typical mass, and dimensions are commercial or typical portion sizes not measured from photographs; estimated uncertainty is ±15–20%. Each row records the mass, shape, and dimensions used in `motivo`. The other 15 densities are recipe calculations (`calculada (receta)`).
- **Decisions:** not verified against external sources. They depend on USDA descriptions and the criteria in `REVISION.md`. The 20-row check yielded a 10% error rate, concentrated in low-confidence rows.
- **Pending technical review:** 48 rows in `revision_tecnica.csv`. These include 23 low-confidence rows, 15 recipes, 21 proxies or approximations, and 5 explained coherence cases.

### Known Limitations

- **Foundation entries with few nutrients** (between 11 and 58 per entry). For ~20 classes, vitamins and minerals are missing. Energy and macronutrients are validated.
- **Recipes with zero-filled nutrients** (between 10 and 328 values per recipe). Primarily affects vitamins and minerals.
- **Proxies:** classes without an exact USDA equivalent (e.g., `aperitif` → alcoholic punch, `halloumi` → paneer). Flagged in `revision_tecnica.csv`.
- **Solid vs. apparent density:** for single pieces (e.g., an apple), the photographed volume includes little air. For the 43 geometric classes, a compact solid shape was used, so the density is solid rather than bulk; if the food is served in a pile, the photographed volume is larger and weight will be overestimated. This can be corrected by weighing or measuring a real portion.
- **Idealized geometry:** typical dimensions do not exactly describe the photographed piece (a croissant is not a perfect ellipsoid; a pizza is not an exact cylinder). Expected error is ±15–20%, sufficient for calorie estimation but not precision weighing.
- **Alcohol:** energy for alcoholic beverages in `Energy (kcal)` comes from USDA. The Atwater formula excludes alcohol; this is not a mapping error.
