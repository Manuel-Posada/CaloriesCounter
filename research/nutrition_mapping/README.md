# Nutritional Mapping of the 323 Classes (Calorie Counter)

This mapping assigns each class in the dataset (`classes.json`) to a food entry from the **USDA FoodData Central** database, with all its nutrients per 100 g, its **density** (g per cm³), and, where applicable, its **recipe** composition (composite dishes).

It enables converting what is seen in the photo (volume in cm³ or portions) into **grams, calories, and macronutrients**.

Status: complete mapping (323/323 classes) and verified on a batch of 20 rows (18/20 correct, 2 errors fixed). All 323 classes have a density value: 203 USDA volume measures, 60 from published tables, 2 physical references, 43 geometrically calculated, and 15 calculated by recipe. **None remain pending weighing**; the 43 geometric ones are flagged and have an estimated uncertainty of ±15–20% (see [Section 3](#3-density-g-per-cm³)). Pending: technical review of 48 nutrient rows.

## 2. How to Use the Mapping

### 2.1 Converting Volume to Grams

```
grams = volume (cm³) × density (g/cm³)
```

**Example (green bean, `idx` 1):** density 0.528 g/cm³.

- 1 USDA cup = 236.6 cm³ → 236.6 × 0.528 = **125 g**. This matches the cup weight published by USDA (125 g), so the value is consistent.

### 2.2 Converting Grams to Calories and Macros

Using the class row in `nutrition_wide.csv`:

```
kcal    = grams / 100 × Energy (kcal)
protein = grams / 100 × Protein (g)
carbs   = grams / 100 × Carbohydrate, by difference (g)
fat     = grams / 100 × Total lipid (fat) (g)
```

**Example (green bean, 125 g):**

| Nutrient | Per 100 g | In 125 g |
|---|---|---|
| Energy | 35 kcal | 43.8 kcal |
| Protein | 1.89 g | 2.4 g |
| Carbohydrates | 7.88 g | 9.9 g |
| Fat | 0.28 g | 0.35 g |

### 2.3 End-to-End Flow (Photo → Result)

1. The vision model identifies the class and estimates the apparent volume (cm³) of each food item.
2. Look up the class in `nutrition_wide_densidad.csv`, multiply the volume by `densidad_g_cm3`, and obtain grams.
3. From the same row, multiply `grams / 100` by `kcal_100g` and by each nutrient.
4. If the volume is a portion in spoons or cups, use `nutrition_map.json` (`portions`) or USDA household measures as an alternative.

If you prefer separate tables, `nutrition_wide.csv` and `densidad_alimentos.csv` join on `idx`.

### 2.4 Composite Dishes

The 15 dishes with `fdc_data_type = receta` (rösti, coleslaw, salad, bolognese, risotto, tarts…) have their nutrients calculated by summing their ingredients by weight. See [Section 4](#4-composite-dishes-recipes).

## 3. Density (g per cm³)

### What It Measures

The density in this mapping is the mass of the food per unit of **apparent volume**—that is, including the air between pieces when the food is in a pile (cooked rice, salad, nuts, flakes). For compact pieces (apple, meat, cheese), it is solid density.

This matters because a photo measures apparent volume. Therefore, the density used corresponds to how the food appears on the plate.

### Where Each Value Comes From

Applied in the following priority order:

1. **USDA volume measure** (`fuente`: *USDA: medida de volumen de la entrada*), **203 classes**. USDA provides the weight of a cup or tablespoon of that food. We divide the weight by the volume (cup = 236.6 cm³; tablespoon = 14.8 cm³; teaspoon = 4.9 cm³, etc.). When multiple measures exist, we take the median, preferring non-chopped measures. Measures that report yields (`yield`), measure a different physical form (whole fruit vs. 1 cup sliced, block vs. shredded), or fall outside the valid range (0.08–1.6 g/cm³) are discarded. Discarded example: hazelnut spread at 2.50 g/cm³, clearly a database error.
2. **Specific FAO/INFOODS entry** (`fuente`: *FAO/INFOODS Density DB v2*), **33 classes**: cooked pasta 0.59; cake 0.415; lasagna 1.04; soup 0.99 and thick soup 1.09; boiled potato 0.59; almonds 0.46; pistachios 0.646; cooked shrimp 0.77; diced bell pepper 0.51; olives 0.65; bread 0.29 and white bread 0.42; among others.
3. **Table 4.2 of *Engineering Properties of Foods*** (`fuente`: *referencia tabulada: tabla 4.2*), **23 classes**: meats 1.055 (published range 1.05–1.07 for 74–84% water), lean fish 1.055, apple 0.85 (range 0.775–0.925), pear 1.00, and cheese 1.08–1.15 (range 1.08–1.23).
4. **Other published tables**, **4 classes**: whole potato 1.06 (USDA AMS specific gravity), beetroot 0.76 and cashews 0.56 (Engineering ToolBox), and nut butter 1.082 (USDA peanut butter measure, used as proxy).
5. **Category physical reference** (`fuente`: *referencia* or *física*), **2 classes**: whole egg 1.03 and cream 0.99.
6. **Geometric calculation** (`fuente`: *cálculo geométrico*, `estado`: `calculada (geometría)`), **43 classes**: no published density exists for the form in which they are served. Calculated as `ρ = mass / volume`, using the USDA portion mass (or a typical mass if USDA does not provide one) and the volume of a solid with typical commercial or serving dimensions: ellipsoid, sphere, cylinder, prism, pizza wedge, cone, or cup. **Dimensions were not measured from the photo**; they are typical sizes. Estimated uncertainty: ±15–20%. See the [complete table](#geometrically-calculated-densities-43).
7. **Recipes** (`fuente`: *fórmula de mezcla sobre ingredientes*), **15 dishes**: mixture density calculated via `ρ = 100 / Σ(gᵢ / ρᵢ)` from each ingredient's density. Marked as *calculada (receta)*.

### Cross-Check with FAO and Table 4.2

Recipe densities are computed using ingredient densities sourced from USDA, FAO, or Table 4.2. The 43 geometric values were checked against the expected physical range (0.25–1.20 g/cm³) and against FAO values for equivalent foods; all fell within range. The review also fixed keyword-based classification bugs: `blueberries` was falling into "blue cheese", `pineapple` into "nuts" (due to *pine*), `potato_salad` into "leafy greens", `pasta_in_butterfly_form_farfalle` into "butter" (due to *butterfly*), `quiche` into "cheese", and `salad_lambs_ear` into "lamb". All 168 changes are logged in `densidad_cambios.csv` with the previous and new values.

### Geometrically Calculated Densities (43)

For these 43 classes there is no published density for the form in which they are served: whole fruit, vegetables, soft cheeses, and composite dishes. Either no USDA volume measure exists, or it measures a different physical form (whole fruit vs. slices, block vs. shredded, whole breast vs. diced). Therefore, density was calculated as:

```
ρ = portion mass / volume of the solid
```

- **Mass:** the USDA portion listed in `nutrition_map.json` ("mass and origin" column). If USDA does not provide a portion, a typical mass is used, as noted in the table.
- **Volume:** formula for a named solid (ellipsoid, sphere, cylinder, prism, pizza wedge, cone, or cup) using typical commercial or serving dimensions.
- **Dimensions were not measured from the photo:** they are typical sizes. The result is a **calculated** density, not a measurement. Estimated uncertainty: **±15–20%**.
- For a cup, 236.6 cm³ is used. For pizza, 30 cm (12") cut into 8 slices; thickness depends on style (thin 1.0 cm, with vegetables 1.4 cm, with ham 1.6 cm). For shredded/mounded meat, a conical mound of 10 cm diameter and 4 cm height. For persimmon (`kaki`) and kiwi, a typical size consistent with the mass (168 g and 75 g) was used: the USDA 2 1/2" dimension for persimmon does not match 168 g, and the 140 g USDA portion for kiwi corresponds to a large fruit.
- Range of the 43 values: **0.259** (croissant) to **1.164** (praline).

There are no longer any densities pending weighing: all 323 have a value. The 43 geometric ones benefit most from a **direct measurement** (`medición propia`), because their dimensions are typical rather than measured from the photo. Simply weigh a real portion (or measure its volume with water / a graduated cylinder) and replace the value, recording `medición propia` in `fuente` and `estado`. The 265 classes with a published or physical source (203 USDA + 60 from tables + 2 references) do not need weighing except for spot checks. The 15 recipes can be refined by weighing the finished dish.

## 4. Composite Dishes (Recipes)

Dishes without a faithful USDA entry (rösti, bolognese, risotto, coleslaw, salads, tarts, glucose drink…) are calculated using `recetas.csv`:

- Each row is an ingredient with its USDA ID and its **grams per 100 g of ready-to-eat dish** (already cooked, as eaten).
- The grams in each recipe sum to 100 g.
- Nutrients: weighted sum of each nutrient by the grams of each ingredient.
- A nutrient not reported by an ingredient counts as 0. This is appropriate for sugars in an oil, but can **underestimate vitamins and minerals** when the data is genuinely missing.
- Energy: if an ingredient only provides Atwater energy (957/958), it is used as regular energy (kcal).
- Typical portion (`porcion_g`): estimated, not measured.

To modify a recipe: edit `Utilidades/recetas.csv` (or the `RECETAS` list in `scripts/merge.py`) and regenerate (see [Section 6.4](#64-how-to-change-a-decision-or-a-recipe)).

## 5. Mapping Criteria

Applied to all classes:

1. **As-consumed state.** Vegetables, meats, fish, pasta, rice, and legumes are mapped cooked. Salads and fruit, raw. (Example: `chicken_breast` → cooked roasted chicken, not raw.)
2. **Respect class qualifiers.** "Unsalted" and "without dressing" are respected.
3. **Composite dishes.** Standard recipe per 100 g of ready-to-eat dish.
4. **Generic classes.** If the class does not specify preparation, the typical preparation as consumed is used.
5. **Between two valid candidates, choose the more generic one** (unbranded, no rare variety).
6. **No brand names.** Branded items are replaced by generic categories (e.g. DiGiorno → generic pizza).
7. **Complete entries.** USDA entries with incomplete macronutrients are discarded (e.g. Foundation grapefruit missing protein and fat).

These criteria are applied in `REVISION.md`.

## 7. Quality and Limitations

### What Is Verified

- **Energy coherence:** 316 of 323 classes satisfy kcal ≈ 4·P + 4·C + 9·F (±15%) with fiber correction. The remaining 7 are explained: wine, beer, and Aperol (alcohol is not in the Atwater formula) and water (0 kcal).
- **Recipes:** all sum to 100 g.
- **Decisions:** all 323 have a decision. None were left pending.
- **Measured error rate:** 2 errors out of 20 verified rows (10%; 95% CI: 2.8%–30.1%). Both were fixed (`spring_roll_fried` and `bread_grain`).

### What Is Not Verified

- **Geometrically calculated densities:** 43 rows with `estado = calculada (geometría)`. **They are not measurements.** Mass comes from a USDA portion or a typical mass, and dimensions are commercial or typical serving sizes, not measured from the photo; estimated uncertainty is ±15–20%. Each row records the mass, shape, and dimensions used in `motivo`. The other 15 densities are recipe calculations (`calculada (receta)`).
- **Decisions:** not verified against external sources. They rely on USDA descriptions and the criteria in `REVISION.md`. The 20-row verification showed a 10% error rate, concentrated in low-confidence rows.
- **Pending technical review:** 48 rows in `revision_tecnica.csv`. These include 23 low-confidence rows, 15 recipes, 21 proxies or approximations, and 5 explained coherence cases.

### Known Limitations

- **Foundation entries with few nutrients** (between 11 and 58 per entry). For ~20 classes, vitamins and minerals are missing. Energy and macronutrients are validated.
- **Recipes with zero-filled nutrients** (between 10 and 328 data points per recipe). Primarily affects vitamins and minerals.
- **Proxies:** classes without an exact USDA equivalent (e.g. `aperitif` → alcoholic punch, `halloumi` → paneer). These are flagged in `revision_tecnica.csv`.
- **Solid density vs. apparent volume:** for whole pieces (e.g. an apple), the photo volume includes little air. For the 43 geometric classes a compact solid shape was used, so the density is solid rather than bulk; if the food is served in a pile, the photo volume is larger and weight is overestimated. This is corrected by weighing or measuring a real portion.
- **Idealized geometry:** typical dimensions do not exactly describe the photographed piece (a croissant is not a perfect ellipsoid, a pizza is not an exact cylinder). Expected error is ±15–20%—sufficient for calorie estimation, not for precision weighing.
- **Alcohol:** energy for alcoholic beverages in `Energy (kcal)` comes from USDA. The Atwater formula does not include alcohol; this is not a mapping error.
